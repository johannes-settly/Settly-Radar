# Discovery interview — Mahshad (17 Nov 2026)

## Signals and targeting
- Besides intent signals (relocation plans, unhappy with vendor), the team also targets strong logos they
  believe they can add value to, even without a signal.
- **Biggest challenge: getting signals**, because the ICP needs many filters (HQ in NL, NL decision-making
  power, FTE). A plain list of NL companies by size is not the ICP.
- Current signal sources are fragmented: Claude, Google Alerts on funding sites, LinkedIn Sales Navigator,
  manual LinkedIn browsing. Paul is trialling Amplemarket (looks strong, includes Phantombuster-style
  LinkedIn engagement signals).

## Her Claude scheduled job
- Weekly; checks given websites (e.g. EU-Startups, Tech.eu for funding news).
- Excludes HubSpot customers, closed-won, nurture and closed-lost; looks at companies not active in pipeline.
- Outputs **top 20 companies** with a signal column ("because X happened").
- Can draft signal-based messages from an instruction set and find leads, but is inconsistent about GDPR
  (sometimes withholds names, sometimes not).
- Quality: **~80% good.** Misses e.g. people who left a company but haven't updated LinkedIn. Sometimes the
  signal is wrong ("we don't need this").
- Deduplication: HubSpot last activity.

## Outreach
- **Chrono** runs email + LinkedIn sequences (used for ~1 year). Sequences per segment, e.g. by FTE cohort.
- Adding a company to a sequence ≈ 2 minutes (find the right lead, add). Chrono syncs to HubSpot.
- Chrono's own signals are poor (e.g. "opened email twice").
- Volume: recently ~20 emails + 20 LinkedIn per day; moving to **~200 people/week** standard (more touches
  per person via cadence).
- **~1% positive reply rate**; when they get a meeting it usually signs.
- Many companies are uncertain ICP; replies are sometimes the test. Re-entering via new hires at accounts
  that never replied is common.
- Experiments: messaging to companies **within their own FTE cohort** performs best. Signal-based campaign
  produced a signed deal and another at final stage. Open to faster A/B testing.
- Exploring a call channel. Amplemarket includes a dialer, but ROI matters: ~€600/month starting price,
  more per user; a cheaper SMB offer is under discussion.

## HubSpot and reporting
- HubSpot is the single source of truth. Lead workflows: emailed → *contacted*; reply → *engaged*;
  negative reply → *nurture* (manual); deal created → *opportunity*; signed → *customer*.
- No reply → stays "contacted" (problem).
- HubSpot can't distinguish "7 touches in one sequence" from "7 separate attempts"; you go back to Chrono.
  HubSpot sequences lack LinkedIn automation, hence Chrono.
- **Reporting is a nightmare:** Chrono analytics are wrong (counts "not interested" as engaged/converted),
  so she rebuilds percentages weekly in Excel from Chrono raw numbers + HubSpot.

## Later funnel
- Deal creation after first meeting takes ~3 minutes but is one more thing to track as meetings scale.
- Pricing: complex; proposals sometimes take hours, sometimes a week of team deliberation. Undermines
  confidence in calls ("we'll get back to you"); no discount authority/flexibility. Subscription model
  confuses prospects. Claude already generates on-brand proposal docs.

## Her ideal end state
> Every Monday, a set of accounts not contacted in the last month, split per person ("this part for Paul,
> this part for me"), in HubSpot or Claude, without going through five tools.
